Mission 1, explained: how a leaderless drone swarm gets a person out of a collapsed building

After an earthquake, the inside of a building is unknown, GPS drops out, and walls block radio. This page walks through the ideas behind the simulation one at a time. For each idea there is a small picture you can play with.

01The scenario

A 200 m × 200 m area. In the middle is a damaged building. Nobody knows its layout, and nobody knows where its openings are. All drones start at launch pad A. A person is somewhere inside, and the goal is to get them to the designated safe area. Nobody is in charge. Every drone decides for itself, using only what it sees and what its neighbours tell it.

Two of the four openings work. One is blocked by rubble inside, and one is blocked by a collapsed facade outside, which you can only see from the street. That's why some drones work inside and others inspect from outside.

02Each drone is a node with a state vector

Every drone carries the same kinds of hardware: a Jetson-class GPU, lidar, thermal and optical cameras, a light, a speaker and a small arm. The quality differs, so each drone is best at something but can still stand in for another. A few times a second, each drone broadcasts a short status message, its state vector:

id · p̂ ± σ (position and how sure it is) · v (velocity) · r (heading) · battery · health · Nᵢ (neighbours) · leaf · root hash

The bars are capability levels from 0 to 1. A task asks for a mix of them (section 05). This overlap is what lets the swarm re-plan when a drone fails.

03Who can talk to whom: the comms graph

Drones are nodes, and radio links are edges. Two drones are linked if they are close enough, and every wall between them shrinks that range:

link if d ≤ min(rᵢ, rⱼ) · k^walls link strength aᵢⱼ = e^(−d²/σ²)

Try it: add a wall and watch the link drop at a distance that was fine in the open. That's why a drone deep inside the building needs others to pass messages along, hop by hop.

04Staying connected: critical links

No drone sees the whole network. It sees its own neighbours, and each neighbour reports its neighbours, so a drone can sketch the graph around it. It then asks one question for each of its links: "if this link broke, could I still reach my teammates some other way?" If the answer is no, the link is critical, and the drone will not fly out of its range.

Look: orange edges are critical, the bridges that hold the network together. A drone held back by a critical link can pull its neighbour along. A drone that loses every neighbour flies to where a teammate was last reported, predicted from its stored message history.

05The task tree, and why it's a Merkle tree

The goal sits at the top, the root. It splits in two, and again, down to concrete leaves a single drone can do: search inside, map, inspect outside, track the person, guide them. Each drone keeps its own copy of this tree. Every node also carries a hash, a 256-bit BLAKE3 fingerprint computed from its children (the diagram shows the first hex digits). Change anything in a leaf, and every hash above it changes, all the way to the root.

Click a leaf in Drone A's copy to mark it claimed or done.

Why this matters: two neighbours first compare just their root hashes, 32 bytes each (BLAKE3, 256 bit). If the hashes match, their trees are identical and nothing needs to be sent. If not, they walk down only the branches whose hashes differ. That saves radio bandwidth, which is scarce inside a collapsed building.

06Claiming a leaf

A free drone scores every open leaf and claims the best one, provided the score passes a threshold. It skips leaves someone else already holds, and it keeps its leaf until that leaf's success criteria are met, for example "≥ 90% of the floor searched" or "person is in the safe area".

score = priority × capability fit × battery factor − distance cost
DuplicatesBecause there's no leader, two drones that heard different news can claim the same leaf. When their trees meet, the better-suited drone keeps it.
SwapsTwo drones in radio range can trade leaves if both end up better matched.
Priority thresholdIf an important leaf stays open for 30 s, the best-suited drone on a less important leaf drops its leaf and takes it.

07Edge cases become local sub-trees

Things go wrong in the field. When a drone runs into a problem on its own leaf, it splits that leaf in two: [keep doing my job, new sub-task]. Then it lets the drones nearby sort out the sub-task. For the first 15 s only drones within 2 hops may claim it. After that, anyone can.

At the edge of its radio linkadds "Relay for me". A backup launches and extends the chain. This is how the swarm decides by itself to send a third or fourth drone inside.
Lost GPSadds "Localize me". A teammate comes close enough to range it.
New debris on the routeadds "Re-map the route". A mapping drone explores the detour.
Leaf donethe sub-tree closes. When every leaf is done, the root is reached: consensus.

08Knowing where you are without GPS

Inside the no-GPS zone, a drone estimates its position from its own motion (dead reckoning). Its uncertainty σ grows the longer and faster it flies. Neighbours with a good fix can pin it back down: two of them measure range through walls (trilateration), or one with a clear line of sight measures range and bearing.

σ grows at 0.03 + 0.04·|v| m/s, until two neighbours range the drone.

09The abort signal

If the goal stops making sense (the building is about to collapse, or the person didn't survive), one drone gets an abort. At every hop, each drone that has it passes it on to the neighbour with the most neighbours first, because that one can spread it furthest. Every drone that receives the abort drops its leaf and flies back to the launch pad.

10Did it work?

The simulation ran headless 20 times: 10 building layouts, one or three people, 4 to 10 drones, and a crash, a navigation fault into the no-go zone, a lidar, radio or battery failure. All 20 runs got everyone to the safe area and reached consensus, in 3 to 10 simulated minutes. The flying network was split for about 5% of flight time, mostly right after a bridge drone crashed, and it repaired itself every time.
